>
> It's a platform for doctor teams: patient management, online consultation, EMR, remote conferences, health management, and a doctor community. Eight modules in all.

> Today we won't just click through features. We'll go requirement by requirement — I'll read the requirement, then show how our system meets it.
>
> So, Requirement 1, secure login. Doctors don't want password-only login. They want email and SMS codes, facial recognition, role-based permissions, temporary permissions for consultations that auto-expire, and different data for different doctors.

>

## Member 2 · Backend + Authorization (2.5 min)

> Hi everyone. I'll show you the backend that makes that security real.
>
> It's NestJS with TypeORM and PostgreSQL, all TypeScript. Code is layered — Controller exposes the API, Service has the logic, Entity maps to a table, DTO validates input.
>
> Let me open Swagger at localhost:3001/api/docs. All our APIs are here, grouped by module, and you can test them right in the browser.
>
> So how does login work? When you log in, we issue a JWT valid for seven days. The front end sends it in every header. A global JwtAuthGuard protects everything, public routes are marked @Public, and a RolesGuard checks roles — so only admins open Access Management.
>
> Watch this. Call a protected endpoint with no token — 401. A normal doctor hits an admin endpoint — 403.
>
> And the real foundation: every query is scoped to the current doctor. Doctor A can never read Doctor B's data.
>
> That same guard layer also writes to the audit log — my teammate covers that later.
>
> That's the backend. Handing over for the front end. Thanks!

**Key points**: backend stack + layering + Swagger → Req 1 enforcement: 7-day JWT + JwtAuthGuard + RolesGuard + @Public, 401/403 demo, query-level data isolation, same guard layer writes audit logs (Req 13) → hand off to frontend.

## Member 3 · Frontend + Patients + Consultations (2.5 min)

> Hi everyone. Now the front end. React 18, TypeScript, Vite, Ant Design, Redux Toolkit. Pages for views, services for API calls, store for state, router for routing. Axios attaches the token, and route guards send you back to login if you're not signed in.
>
> Now Requirement 2, patient management. The requirement: search by name, ID or symptom; view and edit basic info and health records; group patients by disease or status.
>
> Our answer: in the Patients module, you search by name, ID or symptom with pagination. You create and edit a patient's basic info. And every patient has a group and a management status — 在管, 待随访, 已转出 — so you filter and manage similar patients in batches.
>
> Next, Requirement 3, online consultation. The requirement: receive requests, support text-image and HD video with recording, auto-save everything, query and export history.
>
> Our answer: the Consultations module has a clear flow — 待接诊 to 进行中 to 已完成. Each one is 图文 or 视频, with an attachments field for images and a video-recording field. Records auto-save and you can look them up anytime.
>
> Everything's validated twice — front end instantly, backend again. That's it. Handing over for the EMR. Thanks!

**Key points**: frontend stack + structure/guards → Req 2: search by name/ID/symptom, edit basic info, group by disease/status → our search + pagination, edit basic info, group + status batch management → Req 3: text-image + video, recording, auto-save, query/export → our consultation flow + text-image/video + attachments/recording fields + auto-save → hand off to EMR.

## Member 4 · Electronic Medical Records (3 min)

> Hi everyone. Now the core — Requirement 4, electronic medical records. The requirement: structured templates, create-modify-stop orders, senior doctors review juniors, and archive after final review.
>
> Our answer is the full EMR loop. Let me log in as doctor_demo.
>
> I open Medical Records and click Create. We have three structured templates — outpatient, inpatient, physical exam — with fixed fields. I fill in the history and diagnosis, save as draft.
>
> In Orders, I can prescribe three types — medication, lab, exam. Modifying or stopping keeps a full history, and stopping needs a reason. Watch — add an order, change it, stop it with a reason, and expand the row to see the history.
>
> When it's ready, I submit for review and assign a senior doctor. After that, the author can't edit anymore.
>
> Now I switch to senior_demo. I open the record, write an opinion, and either Return it or Approve it. After a return, the author revises and resubmits.
>
> There are hard rules: you can't review your own record, a senior can only review records assigned to them, and anything else returns 403.
>
> After approval, we archive. Archived records are locked — no edit, no delete, and even with the record ID, the API says 403.
>
> That's the whole EMR loop. Handing over for health and conferences. Thanks!

**Key points**: Req 4: structured templates, order create/edit/stop, senior review, archive → our three templates (outpatient/inpatient/physical exam), order history + stop reason, submit for review → senior return/approve + rules (can't self-review, only assigned records, 403) + archive lock → hand off to health/conferences.

## Member 5 · Health Management + Remote Conferences (3 min)

> Hi everyone. I've got two modules — Requirements 5 and 6.
>
> First, Requirement 6, health management. The requirement: personalized plans, real-time monitoring like blood pressure and glucose, automatic reminders, and periodic assessments.
>
> Our answer: a doctor creates a plan with goals, guidance, and an assessment level. It records monitoring data with time and device. Then we generate a patient link valid for seven days — the patient opens it with no account, views guidance, uploads data, reads reminders. Back on the doctor side, it refreshes in about ten seconds.
>
> For reminders, we set "measure blood pressure daily." The backend processes due tasks every fifteen seconds and pushes to the patient portal, even re-sending overdue ones after a restart. And doctors write periodic assessments that update the plan and send new advice.
>
> Now Requirement 5, remote consultation. The requirement: initiate or receive requests, upload records and imaging, bring multiple experts together in real time, and generate the consultation report.
>
> Our answer: a doctor starts a conference and invites up to twenty doctors, filtering by department, including cross-department. Invited doctors accept and submit their department's opinion, and both sides refresh every ten seconds — real-time collaboration. The host summarizes all opinions and closes it, and that becomes the report. One note: a conference only shares the summary, never the full patient record, and uninvited accounts can't see it at all.
>
> That's both modules. Handing over for the community, audit, and conclusion. Thanks!

**Key points**: Req 6: personalized plan, real-time monitoring, auto reminders, periodic assessment → our plan + BP/glucose monitoring + patient link (7 days) + 10s refresh + timed reminders (15s processing, resend after restart) + periodic assessment → Req 5: initiate/receive, upload records/imaging, multi-expert real-time, generate report → our cross-department invites (≤20) + opinion collaboration (10s refresh) + summary as report + data isolation → hand off to conclusion.

## Member 6 · Doctor Community + Audit + Conclusion (2.5 min)

> Hi everyone. Last part — Requirements 7 through 14.
>
> Requirements 7 to 12, the doctor community. It's optional — for professional discussion, isolated from patient data. The requirement: specialty groups, anonymous or real-name discussion, likes, comments, private messages, reporting, and the ability to hide the whole module.
>
> Our answer: the Doctor Community module lets you publish case discussions, browse and search, and like posts. Patient records are never synced automatically — anything you share, you desensitize yourself first. And since it's a separate module, you can hide it and just use consultation, EMR, and conferences without interruption.
>
> Requirement 13, audit. The requirement: log operations like viewing files, editing records, launching consultations; and generate reports by person, time, and type.
>
> Our answer: the Audit module logs every operation — operator, action, target, result, time. Admins can filter by actor, type, result, and keyword.
>
> Requirement 14, user experience. The requirement: smooth interface and complete, secure data. Our answer: clean Ant Design UI, data in PostgreSQL with encrypted passwords and hashed tokens, backed by 161 automated assertions and Playwright browser tests on desktop and mobile.
>
> So, to wrap up: against the full requirement list, we built a complete Doctor Services System — secure login, role and temporary permissions, patient management, consultation, EMR, conferences, health management, an optional community, and audit logging — all deployable with Docker.
>
> That's our demo. Thank you — happy to take questions!

**Key points**: Req 7–12 community: peer exchange, privacy isolation, groups, anonymous/real-name, like/comment/PM, reporting, hideable → our posts/list/like, patient data never auto-synced, manual desensitization, hideable module → Req 13 audit: operation logging, reports by person/time/type → our full logging, filter by actor/type/result/keyword → Req 14 UX: smooth UI, complete secure data → our Ant Design + encrypted passwords + hashed tokens + 161 assertions + Playwright → conclusion.

## ⚠️ Notes for the presentation (internal, not part of the script)

The following requirement points are not fully implemented yet. The script above is worded carefully, but they may come up in Q&A — decide whether to build them or rephrase:

- **Facial recognition** (Req 1): only a reserved `face_model` field, not wired into login.
- **Medical history / allergy records** (Req 2): no dedicated `patient` fields, only shown through EMR.
- **Video consultation + recording** (Req 3): the "video" type and `video_recording` field exist, but no real audio/video is integrated.
- **Consultation export** (Req 3): no export endpoint.
- **Auto-generated conference report** (Req 5): currently the host summarizes manually.
- **Doctor community** (Req 7–12): only posts/list/like; groups, comments, private messages, reporting and moderation are not built.
- **Audit reports** (Req 13): filterable by actor/type/result/keyword, but no date-range filter or report export.
